Reading the card

Some defects hide behind others. You can't see #4 until #3 is fixed, because the compiler stops at the first error. You can't see #9 until the code compiles under the referee's flags. You can't see the Clippy findings until the tests pass, because make ci stops at the first failing job. The defects come in layers, and you peel them one at a time. That's why the advice all along has been to fix one thing and re-run.

Some defects only exist on the referee's machine. #4 is a warning on your laptop and an error in CI. #2 is invisible to anyone who only tests on their own compiler.

Some defects no machine will find. Rows 15–19 pass every judge the kata has. That's the lesson under all the other lessons: the tools raise the floor, and you still have to read the code.
